# Partner Referral Program — Target List & Commission Structure

**Owner:** Orville Matias · **Built with:** Claude · **Date:** August 2026
**Companion docs:** `path-to-4m-h2-2026-gtm-execution.md` (§6 — "channel is the highest-ROI new source") · `base-referral-and-expansion-kit.md` (customer referrals — *this* doc is third-party partners) · `linkedin-outreach-sequence.md`

> **The thesis.** Our buyer (CCO / CISO / COO at a regulated firm) buys on trust and almost never from cold outbound. But a handful of people **already sit in that trust seat** — the firm's compliance consultant, its E&O/cyber broker, its securities counsel, its custodian rep. Each of them is asked "who should handle our IT and cyber?" several times a year and today has no good answer. **One good partner is worth more than an entire cold list.** The goal of this program is 8–12 productive referral partners, each sending 2–4 qualified firms a year.
>
> **Why they say yes (this is the whole pitch):** we do the thing they *can't* do and don't want to own — run and technically enforce the IT/cyber stack — and we make their work look good, because their WSP finally has evidence behind it. We are **complementary, not competitive**, to every partner on this list. Lead with that, not with the commission.

## 1. Commission architecture

Referral fees in this space cluster at **5–20% of first-year ACV** for software/services, and **5–7.5% recurring** in the MSP channel ([Refgrow benchmarks](https://refgrow.com/referral-fees), [Marketopia](https://www.marketopia.com/revenue-streams/)). Our ACVs are large enough that percentage points are real money, so keep the structure simple and generous — three tiers, one page.

| Tier | Partner does | Commission | On a $65K new-logo ACV |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Refer** | Warm intro, then steps back | **10% of Year-1 contract value**, paid on cash collected | **$6,500** |
| **Co-sell** | Intro + joins the Governance Review + vouches | **15% Year-1, then 5% Years 2–3** | **$9,750 + ~$6,500 over 2 yrs** |
| **Embedded / white-label** | Resells us inside their engagement, owns the client relationship | **20–25% margin** on the platform + cyber lines | negotiated per deal |

**Rules that keep it clean:**
- Paid on **collected revenue**, not booked — never on signature.
- **90-day registration window** on an intro'd firm; renews if the deal is actively progressing in HubSpot.
- Existing Centience/Compuwork clients and anyone already in an open pipeline stage are **excluded** — publish this so no one feels cheated later.
- Clawback if the client churns inside 6 months.
- **One page.** A 12-page channel agreement kills a boutique-consultant partnership before it starts.

**The non-cash track — you will need it.** Several of the best partner types are ethically or contractually blocked from taking a commission (see §8). Offer them the same value in a form they *can* accept:
- **Reciprocal referrals** — we send them compliance/legal/insurance work. For a professional-services firm this is usually worth *more* than a fee.
- **Co-branded assessment** — they deliver our Governance Score to their book as their own diagnostic (their brand, our engine). Enormous pull for a consultant.
- **Revenue-neutral bundling** — they buy the platform at partner pricing and bill their client at their own rate.
- **Charitable donation** in the partner's name, or **CE-eligible co-hosted content** — the classic answer for CPAs and attorneys.

## 3. Tier 2 — Risk & professional services around the same client

### Cyber liability / E&O insurance brokers ⭐ *second-highest fit*
Structurally the best-aligned partner in the entire market: **the carrier makes them ask the exact questions we answer** (MFA, EDR, backups, email security, IR plan), and every control we enforce **lowers their client's premium and their own loss ratio**. Custodians and BDs increasingly require cyber coverage as a condition of doing business ([The Coyle Group](https://thecoylegroup.com/cyber-insurance-for-rias/)), so these brokers touch nearly every firm in our ICP.

**Offer:** Refer (10%) plus a **"pre-underwriting readiness" co-offer** — we harden the firm, they place better coverage at a better rate. Ask them to hand our assessment to any applicant who fails the cyber questionnaire. That's a warm, pre-qualified, urgent lead every single time.

### Securities & investment-management law firms
They see the firm at its most exposed moments — formation, an SEC/FINRA deficiency letter, an incident, an enforcement action. A deficiency letter naming cybersecurity or Reg S-P is the highest-intent trigger event that exists for us.

Candidates: [**Stark & Stark**](https://www.stark-stark.com/service/business/investment-management-securities/) · [**Parker MacIntyre**](https://riacompliancelawyer.com/) · [**MGH Law**](https://www.mgh-law.com/) (small/mid BDs and RIAs) · [**My RIA Lawyer**](https://www.myrialawyer.com/services/sec-compliance-lawyer/) · **MarketCounsel/Hamburger** (also Tier 1) · larger practices ([**Morgan Lewis**](https://www.morganlewis.com/our-services/practices/broker-dealer-regulation-compliance), [**Proskauer**](https://www.proskauer.com/practices/broker-dealer)) for enterprise reach.

**Offer:** ⚠️ **Non-cash only** — most bar rules bar paying a lawyer for a recommendation (§8). Use reciprocal referrals, co-authored CLE/webinars, and a standing "remediation partner" slot in their deficiency-response playbook. Lawyers refer generously to vendors who make their remediation letters credible; a fee is not what they want.

### CPA / audit firms serving RIAs and funds
Surprise-custody exams, fund audits, and SOC 2 work put them inside the control environment. Strong for our SOC 2 story.

**Offer:** ⚠️ **Constrained** — AICPA Rule 503 **absolutely prohibits** a commission where the CPA performs audit/review/compilation work for that client, disclosure or not; permissible for non-attest clients **with written disclosure** ([AICPA Code](https://www.njcpa.org/stayinformed/hubs/topics/commissions-and-contingent-fees)). Default to reciprocal referral + co-branded readiness assessment, and let their compliance people decide if a fee is acceptable.

## 8. Legal & regulatory guardrails ⚠️ read before paying anyone

Referral fees are ordinary and legal here, but three partner types carry real constraints. **Have counsel paper the template agreement once**, then reuse it.

| Situation | Constraint | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Paying a CPA/audit firm** | AICPA Rule 503: commission **absolutely prohibited** if they perform audit/review/compilation for that client; permitted for other clients **with written disclosure** ([ref](https://www.njcpa.org/stayinformed/hubs/topics/commissions-and-contingent-fees)) | Non-cash track by default; let their ethics counsel opt in |
| **Paying an attorney** | Bar rules broadly bar giving anything of value for a client recommendation and restrict fee-sharing with non-lawyers | **Never offer cash.** Reciprocal referrals + co-authored content only |
| **Paying an individual registered rep** | [FINRA Rule 3270](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3270): outside compensation from a non-firm source is an outside business activity requiring **written notice to and review by their BD** | Contract with the **firm**, not the individual; require they confirm home-office approval in writing |
| **Paying an RIA or its principal** | Not the SEC Marketing Rule (206(4)-1 governs soliciting *advisory clients*, not vendor referrals) — but a fee from a vendor is a **conflict of interest** the adviser may need to disclose | Put disclosure obligations on the partner in the agreement; require written client disclosure where they recommend us to their own clients |
| **A compliance consultant recommending us to their client** | Independence/objectivity optics — the whole reason the client trusts them | Require **disclosure of the fee to the referred firm.** Non-negotiable. It protects the partner, and a partner who feels exposed stops referring |
| **All partners** | — | Written agreement, no authority to speak for Centience, no client-data access, no guarantees or "compliant"/"certified" claims on our behalf (same discipline as §8 of the GTM plan) |

**Bottom line:** default to **fee + mandatory disclosure** for consultants, brokers, MSPs, platforms and M&A advisors; **non-cash reciprocity** for lawyers and attest-side CPAs.

## 9. Who *not* to pursue

Compliance-software vendors whose product overlaps ours — **COMPLY (ComplySci / RIA in a Box)**, **Smarsh**, **Global Relay**, **Archer**, and similar. Treat as **coopetition**: integration or archiving/surveillance partnerships may make sense (they do communications; we do infrastructure enforcement), but not a referral relationship. Approach only with a specific integration thesis.

## 12. Tracking (HubSpot)

- Create a **Partner** company type with properties: `partner_tier`, `commission_model`, `agreement_signed`, `disclosure_required`, `referrals_sent`, `revenue_attributed`.
- Set **Partner** as an original source on every intro'd deal, with the partner company as an associated record — attribution has to be automatic or fees become an argument.
- **Partner pipeline:** `Identified → Contacted → Meeting held → Agreement signed → First referral → Producing`.
- Monthly: referrals by partner, close rate by partner, commission owed, revenue per partner. Kill inactive partners quietly after two quarters of nothing and reinvest the attention in the ones producing.

## 13. Decisions needed

1. **Commission rates** — are 10 / 15 / 20–25% acceptable against our margins on the MSP + cyber + platform lines?
2. **Recurring vs. one-time** — pay Year-1 only (simpler, cheaper) or 3-year trail (much stronger pull with consultants)?
3. **Disclosure as a hard requirement** — recommended yes, even where not legally required. Confirm.
4. **Healthcare/life-science partners** — hold until vertical enforcement checks exist, or open now selling MSP/cyber only?
5. **Counsel** — who papers the one-page agreement, and can they confirm the CPA/attorney constraints in our states?
