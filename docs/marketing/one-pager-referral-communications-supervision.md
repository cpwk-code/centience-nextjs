## Where we fit

Your compliance programme belongs to your compliance team and your consultant. Policy, regulatory interpretation, your written supervisory procedures and your examination strategy stay with them.

**Centience operates the technology those commitments depend on, and keeps the evidence that they worked.**

That distinction matters, because most findings aren't caused by a missing policy. They're caused by a gap between what the policy promises and what the environment actually enforces — and by nobody being able to show the control operated over a period rather than on the day someone looked.

---

## Communications supervision and archiving

The immediate piece, and the one most firms discover they can't evidence.

- **Capture across the channels business actually happens on** — email, Teams, chat, mobile and the messaging apps staff reach for when email is inconvenient
- **Retention applied by rule and category**, not one blanket period applied to everything and hoped for
- **Review workflow with attestation** — what was sampled, by whom, on what cadence, with what findings, recorded
- **Retrieval on demand** — a request from an examiner, an investor's assessor or counsel becomes a search rather than a project
- **Evidence that the archive is complete**, which is a different question from whether an archive exists

Off-channel communications have been among the most heavily penalised recordkeeping failures of recent years, and the pattern is consistent: the firm archived email thoroughly and treated everything else as informal.

## The layer around it

Once communications are governed, the same engine covers the rest of the technology your programme relies on:

- Access and multi-factor enforcement, tested rather than assumed
- Retention and data classification across the systems holding regulated data
- Device and infrastructure posture
- AI tool inventory and usage — which tools are in use, on whose accounts, and whether the interactions are retained
- Third-party and vendor exposure
- Incident workflow, including the Regulation S-P notification clock

Every finding carries a named owner, the obligation or policy clause it comes from, and a timestamped record of when it was verified.

## Three things we don't do

**We are not your compliance officer.** We don't interpret regulation, own your WSP, or run your exam. Your consultant does that, and we work to them.

**We don't audit our own work.** Where genuine independence is required, an independent assessor consumes our evidence. We will never tell you we are both the builder and the auditor.

**We are not asking you to replace your IT.** Your existing provider stays. We govern the technology regardless of who operates it. Where you authorise us to enforce a specific control domain, that authority is named in writing and reversible.
